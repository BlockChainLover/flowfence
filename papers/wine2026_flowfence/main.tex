\title{FlowFence: Runtime Mediation for Privacy Propagation Externalities in AI-Mediated Platforms}
\titlerunning{FlowFence for Privacy Propagation Externalities}
\makeatletter\def\@author{}\def\@institute{}\makeatother
\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
AI-mediated online platforms increasingly rely on LLM agents that act for different users, teams, vendors, and organizational roles. In such multi-principal workflows, privacy failures are not limited to unsafe final answers: a locally useful write to memory, a workspace, a message, or a tool channel can impose downstream exposure costs on other principals before any final-output filter observes the leak. Existing output filters and channel-level access control miss this propagation externality because risk depends on provenance, fanout, privilege transition, recipient role, and the abstraction level authorized by policy. We present FlowFence, a runtime mediation mechanism that intercepts information-moving events before they update shared state or reach external channels. FlowFence scores propagation risk from runtime-observable policy, provenance, topology, privilege, and semantic-request signals, then allows the event, emits a validated safe view, quarantines raw content, blocks the transition, or narrows its propagation rights. Across retrieval-memory and multi-agent workflow evaluations, FlowFence preserves deterministic task success and eliminates unauthorized raw and external leakage in the evaluated FlowFence runs. Topology analysis shows broader blackboard-style sharing increases leakage and cascade size under no defense, while label-free validation shows containment does not rely on evaluator-only attack annotations. These results support runtime mediation as a practical platform mechanism for governing privacy externalities in agent-mediated online work.
\keywords{AI-mediated platforms \and privacy propagation \and runtime mediation \and multi-agent LLM systems \and platform governance}
\end{abstract}
