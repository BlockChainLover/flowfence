\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent LLM systems increasingly coordinate through shared memory, shared workspaces, inter-agent messages, tool arguments, and final outputs. These channels make privacy failures a propagation problem: sensitive data can be copied into shared state or high-privilege contexts before any final answer is checked. We propose FlowFence-Lite, a non-oracle runtime containment layer for text-first multi-agent systems. FlowFence-Lite mediates information-moving events, scores risk from runtime-observable policy, provenance, topology, fanout, and privilege signals, and routes unsafe content through safe-view rewriting or quarantine. We formalize two containment properties: a policy-preserving safe-view invariant and a fanout-based propagation bound that motivates topology-aware risk scoring. We evaluate FlowFence-Lite on an adapted AgentPoison retrieval-memory comparator, deterministic multi-agent propagation benchmarks, MiniMax-backed synthetic-runtime coverage, and held-out non-oracle validation. In the canonical MiniMax-only 252-run coverage over a synthetic deterministic runtime, FlowFence-Lite is clean across all 63 configured FlowFence runs, with task success 1.0 and zero raw/external leakage. In non-oracle validation, the defense ignores attack labels and still completes 540/540 deterministic runs and 72/72 targeted MiniMax-backed runs with zero raw/external leakage and zero oracle-annotation violations. The evidence supports scoped containment claims for configured MiniMax-backed and deterministic synthetic-runtime settings, while non-MiniMax generalization, production safety, real browser/desktop deployment, and arbitrary attack robustness remain explicit limitations.
\end{abstract}
